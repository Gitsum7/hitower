Research statement

Jack Knight leads HiTower Institute's research program in the earth and environmental sciences. The program studies how natural hazards (severe storms, flooding, wildfire weather and coastal events) reach communities that sit outside conventional infrastructure, and how forecasts, maps and public information can reach those communities sooner. Work is organized as three active research directions, established in 2026. Methods, data sources and findings will be published openly on this site as they are completed.

01 // Research direction

Natural-hazard & severe-weather forecasting

Localized storm, flood and fire-weather forecasting and alerting for under-served and off-grid communities, built on public meteorological and hazard data. Includes HiTower's modular Disaster Forecast tool. Active.

02 // Research direction

Geospatial field & coastal-access studies

Field mapping of trails and public lands, and a study of public access along San Diego's coastal land informed by California Public Records Act requests. Active.

03 // Research direction

Emergency management & public-safety information

How open, public-source information can support disaster response and community safety, including documented methods for verifying public hazard reports. Active.
